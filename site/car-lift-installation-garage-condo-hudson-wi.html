Car Lift Installation in Hudson, WI | Troy Condo Garages

Owner's Guide · Hudson, WI

Troy Condo Garages · Published July 8, 2026

If you own more than one vehicle worth protecting, a car lift is the single upgrade that changes what a garage condo can do for you. Instead of parking one car and leaving the rest of your floor space empty, a lift lets you stack vehicles vertically — doubling your storage capacity in the same footprint. At Troy Condo Garages in Hudson, WI, the 14′–18′ ceilings and thickened concrete slabs were built with exactly this kind of upgrade in mind.

This guide walks through what you need to know before installing a car lift in your garage condo: ceiling clearance, lift types, floor requirements, electrical needs, and what to check before you buy.

Why Garage Condos Are Built for Car Lifts

Most residential garages simply cannot support a car lift. Standard 8′ ceilings do not leave enough overhead clearance, and typical 4-inch residential slabs are not rated for the point-loading a lift puts on the floor. Garage condo developments solve both problems from the ground up:

  • Tall overhead clearance — 14′ to 18′ ceilings comfortably fit a raised vehicle underneath a lift
  • Thickened concrete slab — engineered to handle the concentrated weight of a loaded two-post or four-post lift
  • 150-amp electrical service — enough capacity for a hydraulic lift's motor along with lighting, tools, and a battery charger

Types of Car Lifts for a Garage Condo

Not every lift makes sense for every unit. The right choice depends on your ceiling height, floor space, and how often you need full access underneath the vehicle.

Two-Post Lifts

The most popular choice for a garage condo. Two-post lifts take up the least floor space, give full underside access for maintenance work, and are available in asymmetric configurations that make door-opening easier in tighter units.

Four-Post Lifts

Best for pure storage rather than maintenance access. A four-post lift is essentially a parking platform — drive the car up, raise it, and park a second vehicle underneath. This is the go-to option for collectors who want to store, not wrench.

Scissor & Low-Rise Lifts

A good fit for units with slightly lower usable ceiling height after a mezzanine build-out, since these lifts have a lower maximum rise than post lifts.

What to Check Before You Install

  1. Confirm ceiling height at the specific bay location, since roof pitch can reduce clearance near the edges of a unit.
  2. Verify slab thickness and rebar spec with the installer — Troy Condo Garages units are built with this in mind, but every lift manufacturer has anchor bolt torque and slab requirements to confirm.
  3. Plan your electrical run so the lift motor is on its own circuit, separate from lighting and tool outlets.
  4. Leave clearance for door swing on a raised vehicle, particularly with a two-post lift.

Car Lifts as a Long-Term Investment

Because a garage condo is real property you own — not a rented storage unit — a permanently installed lift is an improvement that adds to your unit's value rather than a fixture you have to leave behind. For collectors building out a private workshop in Hudson, WI, a lift is often the first major upgrade after move-in, right alongside epoxy flooring and mezzanine storage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I install a two-post car lift in a Troy Condo Garages unit?

Yes. Units are built with 14 to 18 foot ceilings and a thickened concrete slab designed to support the point-loading of a two-post or four-post lift. Confirm exact bay dimensions with the team before ordering.

Do I need a permit to install a car lift in my garage condo?

Requirements vary by manufacturer and equipment type. Most owners work with a licensed lift installer who pulls any required electrical or structural permits as part of the installation.

Will a car lift affect my HOA or insurance?

A permanently installed lift is considered a unit improvement. Talk with your insurance provider about updating your policy to reflect the added equipment and stored vehicle value.

How much clearance do I need above a raised vehicle?

Plan for at least 6 to 12 inches above the tallest vehicle you intend to lift, in addition to the lift's own rise height, to avoid contact with ceiling framing or lighting.

The Garage Condo Buying Process: A Step-by-Step Timeline From Reservation to Move-In

Buying Guide · Hudson, WI · Troy Condo Garages

Once you've decided a garage condo is right for you, the next question is almost always the same: what actually happens between now and getting the keys? How to buy a garage condo isn't complicated, but it does follow a different sequence than buying a house or renting a storage unit. Here's the exact process, step by step, based on how buyers move through it at Troy Condo Garages.

Step 1: Define What You're Storing

Before you look at floorplans, get specific about use case. A single classic car needs a very different footprint than a boat-and-trailer combo or a full workshop with a lift. If you haven't already, it helps to review our guides on specific use cases — RV storage, boat storage, or private workshop builds — to get a realistic sense of the size you need.

Step 2: Request Floorplans and Current Inventory

This is where most buyers start: filling out a short form specifying garage purpose (vehicles, RV, boat, gym, hobby, or collection). Our team responds with current Phase I floorplans and pricing. You can start this step directly from our Our Units page.

Step 3: Schedule a Tour

Photos and floorplans only tell part of the story. Ceiling height, drive-aisle width, and door clearance are things you feel standing inside the shell. Most buyers schedule an in-person tour before committing — you can book one anytime by calling the number below.

Step 4: Choose Your Unit and Reserve It

Once you've picked a size and location within the facility, a reservation deposit secures your spot in the current phase. Submitting the inquiry form does not obligate you to purchase — a team member walks you through reservation terms before any deposit is collected.

Step 5: Arrange Financing (If Needed)

Garage condos aren't financed like a typical home mortgage. Some buyers pay cash; others use specialty lenders. We've broken down the available paths in our financing guide, and current options are also listed on our Financing Options page. This step typically runs in parallel with the paperwork below, not after it.

Step 6: Purchase Agreement and Title Work

Because a garage condo is real property, the paperwork looks like a standard real estate transaction: a purchase agreement, title search, and closing disclosures. This stage typically involves a title company and takes several weeks, similar to closing on a home. Budget for closing costs and understand how St. Croix County property taxes will apply to your new unit going forward.

Step 7: Review HOA Documents

Every unit is part of the community HOA, which covers insurance on your space "from the studs out," shared road and grounds maintenance, and security infrastructure. Read through our HOA fees breakdown before closing so there are no surprises about what's included.

Step 8: Closing Day

Closing follows standard real estate procedure: final walkthrough, signing, and recording the deed in your name. From this point forward, the unit is yours — real property you can insure, customize, and eventually sell.

Step 9: Move In and Customize

Most owners treat move-in as the starting line, not the finish line. This is when buyers start planning epoxy flooring, mezzanine build-outs, car lift installation, or climate control upgrades. Our Ultimate Guide to Premium Garage Condos is a good next read once you're through closing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does the entire buying process take?

Most buyers move from initial inquiry to closing in roughly 6–10 weeks, though timelines vary depending on financing and title work.

Does submitting an inquiry form obligate me to buy?

No. Submitting the inquiry form simply starts a conversation with our team and does not obligate you to purchase a unit.

Do I need a real estate agent to buy a garage condo?

It's not required, though some buyers choose to work with one, especially if they're also financing the purchase. Our team can walk you through the process directly either way.

What is a reservation deposit for?

It secures your chosen unit within the current phase while the purchase agreement, financing, and title work are completed.

Can I customize the unit before closing?

Customization typically happens after closing, once the deed is recorded in your name, though you can plan and design your build-out in advance.

What happens at closing?

Closing includes a final walkthrough, signing of closing documents, and recording of the deed in your name, similar to a standard real estate closing.

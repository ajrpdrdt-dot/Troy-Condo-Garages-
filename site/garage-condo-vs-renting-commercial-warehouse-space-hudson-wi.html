Garage Condo vs. Renting Commercial Warehouse Space in Hudson, WI

Buying Guide · Hudson, WI · Troy Condo Garages

Every dollar you spend renting warehouse space is a dollar you'll never see again. That's the blunt math behind a question we hear constantly from contractors, small business owners, and serious hobbyists in the St. Croix Valley: should I keep renting warehouse space, or buy a garage condo in Hudson, WI instead? Let's break down the real numbers, not just the emotional pitch.

What Warehouse Space Actually Costs in 2026

Nationally, small-bay industrial and warehouse space is renting for roughly $7 to $22 per square foot annually, with small-format spaces under 50,000 square feet commanding a 15-35% premium over large distribution warehouses because demand for smaller bays is tight. Even in more affordable interior markets, a modest 1,500-square-foot bay can easily run $1,000-$2,000+ per month once you factor in the base rent plus triple-net (NNN) charges for taxes, insurance, and common area maintenance.

Here's the part landlords don't advertise: that number climbs every single year. Most commercial leases include a 2-5% annual rent escalation clause, meaning your $1,500/month bay today could easily be $1,800+/month by the end of a five-year lease — and at the end of it, you own nothing.

The math tends to favor ownership the longer you plan to occupy the space. If you know you'll need dedicated storage, workshop, or business space for more than three to five years, renting essentially guarantees you'll pay tens of thousands of dollars with nothing to show for it at the end.

Where Renting Still Makes Sense

To be fair, renting isn't always the wrong call. It can make sense if:

  • You need a very short-term space (under 12 months) for a specific project.
  • Your business needs are still evolving and you're not sure how much square footage you'll need long-term.
  • You don't have the down payment or credit profile to qualify for financing yet.

But for most collectors, contractors, and small business owners who know they need permanent, secure, climate-ready space, the long-term math strongly favors ownership.

Running Your Own Numbers

Here's a simple way to compare your specific situation:

  1. Take your current (or quoted) monthly warehouse rent.
  2. Multiply by 60 (five years) and add a conservative 3% annual escalation.
  3. Compare that total to the estimated monthly mortgage payment on a garage condo of similar size, using current garage condo financing rates.
  4. Factor in that at the end of five years, the rental total is gone — while the ownership scenario leaves you with equity and a real, sellable asset.

For most buyers in Western Wisconsin and the Twin Cities metro, that comparison isn't close.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a garage condo cheaper than renting warehouse space?
Monthly payments can be comparable to or even lower than a comparable leased bay once you factor in NNN charges on a lease, and unlike rent, your garage condo payment builds equity instead of disappearing.

Can I run a small business out of a garage condo?
Yes, many owners use their units for hobby businesses, detailing, mobile trades, and storage-based operations. Review our garage condo for small business storage guide for specifics.

What happens if I outgrow my garage condo?
Because you own the unit, you can sell it and use the proceeds toward a larger unit or a different property — unlike a lease, where you simply walk away with nothing.

Do garage condos include insurance like a commercial lease might?
The HOA fee at Troy Condo Garages includes insurance on the structure from the studs out; you'll want a separate policy for your personal property and equipment inside.

Is financing a garage condo harder than signing a commercial lease?
It requires qualifying for a loan, but many buyers find the process comparable to or easier than negotiating a multi-year commercial lease with personal guarantees.

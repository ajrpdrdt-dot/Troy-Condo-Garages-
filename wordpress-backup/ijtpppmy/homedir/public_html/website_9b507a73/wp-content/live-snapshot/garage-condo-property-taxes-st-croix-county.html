Garage Condo Property Taxes in St. Croix County Explained

Buying Guide · Hudson, WI · Troy Condo Garages

Buying a garage condo means buying real property — and real property in Wisconsin comes with property taxes. If you're comparing a garage condo purchase to renting a storage unit or warehouse bay, understanding what you'll actually owe St. Croix County each year is a critical piece of the math. Here's a straightforward breakdown of how garage condo property taxes work in St. Croix County, Wisconsin.

How Property Taxes Are Calculated in St. Croix County

Wisconsin doesn't use one flat statewide property tax rate. Instead, your bill is based on your property's assessed value multiplied by a combined "mill rate" made up of levies from the county, your municipality, your school district, and any special districts serving your property (like fire protection or sanitary districts).

According to county-level property tax data, St. Croix County collects property tax at an effective rate of roughly 1.4% to 1.49% of a property's assessed fair market value on average, which is on the higher end compared to many other Wisconsin counties, but still below top-ranked counties like Dane County. The exact combined rate that applies to a garage condo at Troy Condo Garages depends on which municipality and school district the property falls within.

What This Means in Real Dollars

Using the county's typical effective rate as a rough guide, a garage condo unit assessed at $150,000 might generate an estimated annual property tax bill in the general range of $2,100-$2,250, though your actual bill will vary based on the specific taxing jurisdictions that apply to the property and any assessment changes. This is only a ballpark estimate — always request the actual current assessed value and mill rate for a specific unit from the St. Croix County Treasurer's office before finalizing your purchase decision.

How Garage Condo Taxes Compare to Renting

One thing worth remembering: when you rent a warehouse bay or storage unit, you're not paying property tax directly — but you are paying for it indirectly, since commercial landlords build property tax costs into your triple-net (NNN) lease charges. The difference is that as a renter, you get no equity or ownership benefit in exchange for covering that cost. As a garage condo owner, your property tax payment supports an asset that's building equity for you, not your landlord.

When Property Taxes Are Due

St. Croix County collects property taxes in arrears, similar to income tax timing, and offers both full and installment payment options. According to the official St. Croix County property tax page, payments can be made online, by mail, in person, or via secure drop box at the Government Center in Hudson. Missing an installment deadline can trigger interest and penalties, so it's worth setting calendar reminders or escrowing your property tax payment through your mortgage lender if you finance your unit.

Ways to Stay on Top of Your Garage Condo Tax Bill

  • Escrow through your lender. If you finance your garage condo, ask whether your lender can collect and pay property taxes automatically as part of your monthly payment.
  • Confirm your assessed value annually. Assessments can change; review your notice each year and contact the assessor's office with questions.
  • Ask about the homestead exemption rules. Since garage condos are non-residential, standard homestead exemptions for primary residences typically won't apply — confirm your unit's classification with the assessor.
  • Keep records for tax season. Property taxes on real estate are generally deductible on federal returns as an itemized deduction, subject to current IRS limits — consult a tax professional for your specific situation.

Property Taxes Are Only Part of Your Ownership Costs

Property taxes are just one line item in the total cost of owning a garage condo. Be sure to also factor in your HOA fees, insurance costs, and your mortgage payment when comparing total ownership costs to renting or building on owned land.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are garage condo property taxes higher than a house?
Not necessarily — garage condos are typically assessed based on their actual value and use, similar to other real property. Ask the county assessor for the specific classification and estimated assessment on any unit you're considering.

Who pays property taxes on a garage condo — me or the HOA?
Individual unit owners are typically responsible for property taxes on their specific unit, separate from HOA dues, which cover shared insurance and maintenance. Confirm this structure with Troy Condo Garages directly.

Can I appeal my garage condo's assessed value?
Yes. Wisconsin property owners can generally appeal an assessment they believe is inaccurate through the local Board of Review process; check with the St. Croix County Assessor's office for deadlines and procedures.

Do property taxes increase every year?
Assessments and mill rates can change annually based on municipal, school, and county budget needs. Review your annual assessment notice for updates.

Is property tax included in my HOA fee?
No. HOA fees typically cover shared insurance, road maintenance, and common area upkeep, while property taxes are billed separately by the county to each unit owner.
